Peoples of past cycles

Each past cycle had its own people. Most of them are known only from ruins and old stories. A page opens when you kill that cycle's dragon.

Famous figures

The people who rule this age, and those of past ages whose names survive. Most of the old names are known from a single diary, so this page grows as you find them.

The council of Dilecta

The three councillors are elected by the guilds, from a pool of old houses.

Ambrose Goldmere

Councillor and master of money and trade. Old money, from a family that owns the harbour tolls.

Maren Gatewarden

Councillor and commander of the army. From a lesser old house. Started as a watch guard on the city gates, and never lets anyone forget it.

Aldys Ashless

Councillor and Beacon of Dilecta, the Bright Path's high priest in the capital. A shady background. He has been in the order since he was 12.

Heads of the faiths

Evane Lightsworn

The Dawnbringer, head of the Bright Path, who sits in Restena. A former Lantern from a poor temple, chosen for her fierce faith. She has sworn Restena will be first through the gate of the realm of the Flame.

Arbiter Tessar

Head of the Primacy, speaking for all four Powers. The 4 Elders choose one of their own for life, and he sits in Thornner, the faith's old home. His title comes from an old word for "four". He has no family name, and his old name is secret.

The four Elders of the Primacy

Flame: Ignace Ashvale. Tide: Merrin Deepwater. Stone: Garth Rockhold. Gale: Tobin Highwind. Each speaks for one Power of the Primacy.

Teachers of the past

Elder Maud

A past Elder of the Primacy, named in a shrine: she taught that the Turning comes when the four fall out of balance, and sent a Seeker to sweep the shrine for asking who keeps the scales.

Heroes

Heroes are famous people of this cycle. Each appears once per world with a 3-step request: arrive and ask, do the deed, return. Then they join you and take a slot. Each has 3 good traits, Wealth 5, and one unique offer.

Corvin Ironvow

Retired Lord Commander of Restena, a Walker.

Request: defend a raid. Offer: a Superior shield or sword, weekly.

Aveline Saltmere

Merchant princess of Swetren, a Prima.

Request: fill 3 caravan wanted lists. Offer: one far-land luxury, weekly.

Brannoc Deepdelve

Miners' guild master of Thornner, Keeper of Stone.

Request: mine 128 deepslate ores. Offer: ore blocks 25% cheaper.

Mother Elsbeth

Keeper of a plague ward.

Request: cure 3 zombie villagers. Offer: golden carrots and healing potions.

Tamsin Quickfoot

Smuggler queen.

Request: steal back her ledger from a pillager outpost. Offer: one never-sold item, weekly.

Odo Archwright

Master mason of Dilecta's aqueducts, a Walker.

Request: restore 3 ruined shrines. Offer: rare blueprint pages.

Ysolde Brightwick

Temple-fire Lantern, a Walker.

Request: bring 3 blaze rods. Offer: candles, soul lanterns, Fire Resistance.

Mael Stormwake

Sea captain of Oscat, Keeper of the Tide.

Request: dig up a shipwreck's treasure. Offer: treasure maps, nautilus shells.

Garrick Thornwood

Northern ranger, Keeper of Gale.

Request: kill a roaming elite. Offer: rare-block cache maps.

Lisette Vane

Copper golem factory engineer, who wants them freed.

Request: build a copper golem and keep it 3 days. Offer: copper golem statues, redstone parts.

Legends

Legends were touched by past cycles or by the truth. Each one appears only after a given dragon, and each unlocks one extra lore fragment. Like Heroes, each appears once per world.

Cities and villages

Dilecta

The capital of the World, on the north coast of Trenec. All five continents send taxes and envoys to its council of 3, but they run themselves and resent it. All faiths compete in the city, and the Bright Path holds the most coveted positions.

Setting & Geography: A northen city positioned between mountains, bordered by the ocean on one side and the cold on the other.
Architecture: A hybrid aesthetic blending classical Roman monumental structures, functional Medieval fortifications, and ornate Renaissance facades, combined with steampunk-medieval technology.
Massive aqueducts for mountain-to-cove water delivery.
An extensive subterranean sewer system.
Elevated transit bridges reserved for the city's upper caste.
An underground industrial factory run by captured/enslaved copper golems.
Huge fields outside the city walls, where most farmers live, with a couple houses near the gates.
There's a kind of city center, older, and the different districts.
The port in the north is outside the walls, but it's massive, with quays and canals.
The city is ruled by a council of 3.
Rigid Caste System: Clear social hierarchy dividing the elite ruling class, common citizens, and subterranean labor.

See also: Offices and trades of the city.

Its council: Ambrose Goldmere, Maren Gatewarden and Aldys Ashless.

Oscat

How everything else is named

The mod reads your world and groups big areas into named places: mountain ranges, forests, deserts, seas, rivers and landmasses. They are named as you approach. Names show as entry titles, map labels, a Places page in the Codex, and in villager backgrounds. Vanilla structures keep their names. Ruined shrines carry their own name and fragment. Offices and trades of the city

The positions and jobs of Dilecta, in the author's own words. No one here has a name yet.

Urban Governance & Leadership

The Ruling Council (Triumvirate): The three high councilors holding supreme executive and legislative authority over the city.
Councilors' Aides & Secretaries: Scribes, personal heralds, and administrative assistants managing daily decrees.
Master of the Mint: High official responsible for currency coinage, treasury management, and city taxes.
Chancellor of the Exchequer / Auditor: Bureaucrats tracking state expenditure, tariffs, and guild dues.
Magistrates & Judges: Jurists presiding over civil disputes, petty crimes, and high-court trials.

Military & Security Forces

Lord Commander of the Army: General officer directing garrison troops, external defense, and military campaigns.
Regimental Captains & Lieutenants: Officers commanding infantry, cavalry, or siege engineer units within the royal army.
City Watch Commander (Castellan/Captain of the Watch): Head of urban law enforcement responsible for night patrols, gates, and curfews.
Watchmen & Beat Patrols: Ordinary guards manning the walls, gates, and city districts to deter crime.
Private Bodyguards & Retainers: Elite armsmen sworn directly to individual members of the ruling council.
Bailiffs & Wardens: Specialized officers collecting fines, executing warrants, and overseeing specific city wards.

Religious Hierarchies (Two Major, One Minor)

High Prelates / Arch-Priests: Supreme spiritual leaders of the two major faiths, wielding vast political and moral influence.
Dogmatic Inquisitors / Zealots: Religious enforcers hunting heresy, unapproved spellcasting, and corruption.
Temple Priests, Curates & Clerks: Officiants managing daily rites, marriages, funerals, and temple tithes.
Faith Militant / Templar Knights: Dedicated holy orders serving the major religions as armed defenders.
Heretical Cult Acolytes / Secret Custodians: Underground operatives preserving the tenets of the minor, marginalized faith.
Alms-Givers & Hospitalers: Monastic caretakers running soup kitchens, plague wards, and orphanages.

Commerce, Guilds & Crafts

Guild Masters: Heads of influential trade associations (blacksmiths, weavers, masons) regulating prices and apprentices.
Merchant Princes & Caravan Factors: Traders handling bulk imports, exports, and high-value luxury goods.
Master Masons & Architects: Engineers directing stone fortifications, cathedral building, and structural repairs.
Alchemists & Apothecaries: Brewers of potions, healers, and compounders of chemical reagents or explosives.
Scribes, Illuminators & Printers: Producers of legal documents, scrolls, and early printed texts.
Innkeepers, Taverners & Brothel Keepers: Proprietors managing the city's hospitality and entertainment districts.

Municipal Infrastructure & Underworld

Master of Works / City Engineer: Overseer of aqueducts, sewers, bridges, and public sanitation.
Executioner / Torturer: Municipal official carrying out capital punishment and interrogations.
Rat-Catchers & Night-Soil Men: Laborers clearing waste, vermin, and biological hazards from the streets.
Smuggler Bosses & Fence Operators: Underworld figures running illicit black markets, contraband, and bribes.
Thieves' Guild Enforcers / Cutpurses: Outlaws operating in the shadows of the city's poorest wards.

Faiths and the Vitera

Three faiths are found on every continent, though not equally strong, respected or tolerated everywhere. Each reads the ending of the world through its own beliefs. A fourth group, the Vitera, is hunted by all three.

GroupWhereThe cycle, in its words

The PromiseEverywhere, tolerated but bulliedThe Judgment
The ViteraHunted by all three

The Primacy

The elemental faith. Its followers are called Primas. There are four Powers: Flame, Tide, Stone and Gale. The cycle is "the Turning", the elements washing the world clean when people grow proud.

Ranks: Seeker → Initiate → Keeper (sworn to one element, such as Keeper of the Tide) → Warden (runs a shrine) → Elder (4, one per element) → the Arbiter (speaks for all four).

Texts: the Primacy shrines. People: Aveline Saltmere, Brannoc Deepdelve, Mael Stormwake, Garrick Thornwood, Elder Maud.

Its Elders: the four Elders.

Its head: Arbiter Tessar.

The Bright Path

The faith of light. Its followers are called Walkers. The Bright Flame stands against the Lie, with eternal temple fires and the creed "good thought, good word, good deed". The cycle is "the Ordeal".

Ranks: Spark → Candle → Lantern (keeps a temple fire) → Beacon (high priest of a city) → the Dawnbringer (head of the faith, in Restena).

Texts: the Bright Path shrines. People: Corvin Ironvow, Odo Archwright, Ysolde Brightwick.

Its head: Evane Lightsworn, the Dawnbringer.

The Promise

The small faith. Its followers are called the Promised. The One promised to save a faithful few, through prophets and a Book, and they wait for a promised one. The cycle is "the Judgment". It is tolerated but bullied almost everywhere, and many of its circles meet in hiding.

Ranks: Listener → Believer → Reader (may read the Book aloud) → Shepherd (leads a hidden circle) → the Voice (a prophet; nobody knows if one lives today).

Texts: the Promise shrines.

The Vitera

A cult, hunted by all three faiths. Nobody listens to or trusts them.

Texts: the Vitera shrines.

Structures

Every place you can find in the world, with what the Codex says about it and what is known of who built it.

Abandoned Camp

A cold campfire and scattered gear. Someone left in a hurry.

Nobody ever comes back for their things.

Amethyst Geode

Amethyst inside smooth basalt and calcite. Mine carefully.

Geodes hum at night, miners swear.

Ancient City

A deepslate city in the dark. Walk softly; something listens.

Built around a gate of its own, by people who went too deep.

Bastion Remnant

A piglin stronghold of blackstone and gold.

Built by someone older than the piglins, the fortress diaries say.

Buried Treasure

X marks the spot: a chest, and a heart of the sea.

The Heart was hidden on purpose, a Tidefolk wrote.

Desert Pyramid

A tomb in the sand. Step on the blue tile and you join it.

A Kilnfolk tomb: a prayer made of brick.

Desert Well

A small well in the sand. Brush the sand around it.

Travellers drop a coin in, for luck that never comes.

The cursed dead

Zombie

Spawns in the dark, at night, in caves and dungeon spawners; joins raids and village sieges; burns in sunlight unless it wears a helmet; turns villagers into zombie villagers.

In the Codex

Slow, stubborn, always hungry. Burns at dawn unless it finds a hat.

The Promised call them "the uncounted".

What people say

Nobody mourns a zombie. The old ones say it earned its walk.

A hint

Of all things, it fears the sun, as a thief fears being seen.

Zombie Villager

Born when zombies kill villagers (Normal and Hard); also found in some structures; can be cured with Weakness and a golden apple, then trades much better.

In the Codex

Cure it with a splash of Weakness and a golden apple.

Cured, it remembers you, and trusts you completely.

What people say

A zombie villager still knows your face. That is what makes it so sad to meet.

A hint

It groans in the old voice. Part of someone is still in there, listening.

Husk

Deserts, day and night.

In the Codex

A desert zombie that does not burn by day, and leaves you hungry.

Kilnfolk tombs keep their dead. Not all of them.

What people say

Desert folk say the husk walks because the sand would not take its bones.

A hint

Day does not burn it. It was judged under this very sky, and the sky has nothing left to say.

Drowned

Oceans, rivers, underwater ruins, mostly at night.

In the Codex

Zombies of the deep. Some throw tridents; some hold nautilus shells.

The Tidefolk, the stories say, never stopped guarding.

What people say

Fishers say the drowned walk the seabed looking for the boat they never reached.

A hint

The tide never takes them. It only holds them, like a hand that will not open.

Zombie Horse

In the Codex

A horse that came back wrong.

Ridden by the dead on dark plains, travellers say.

What people say

A hint

It is the only dead thing that looks sorry.

In the Codex

A rotting nautilus, ridden by drowned in the deep.

Camel Husk

Deserts, under a husk rider.

In the Codex

A camel long dead, still walking the dunes.

The Kilnfolk buried their camels beside them. Some never stayed buried.

What people say

Caravan drivers count their camels at dusk and again at dawn, and say nothing of the one too many.

A hint

It walks the old caravan roads, as if it still had a load to deliver.

Allay

Caged at pillager outposts and woodland mansions; peaceful.

In the Codex

Hand it an item and it gathers more of the same, bringing them to a note block you play.

Freed from pillager cages, it hums as if it remembers other cages.

A hint

It hums a tune nobody has written yet.

Bogged

Parched

Deserts.

In the Codex

A sun-dried skeleton that shoots tipped arrows.

Wither Skeleton

Nether fortresses.

In the Codex

Tall and black; its blade withers. Sometimes drops its skull.

They still guard the halls of a king who lost his mind.

What people say

Its blade rots, as the king's court did, slowly and politely.

Skeleton Horse

Thunderstorms, as a skeleton trap.

In the Codex

Comes with the lightning, carrying four riders.

Tamed after the fight, it swims well and fears nothing.

What people say

Creeper, spiders, slime, silverfish

Creeper

Surface at night and in the dark; explodes; drops gunpowder.

In the Codex

Silent, then hissing, then gone, along with your wall.

Cats terrify it. Nobody has ever learned why.

What people say

Nobody has heard a creeper arrive. Only what it left.

A hint

It does not hate your house. It cannot stand anything that stays built.

Spider

Surface at night and caves; calm in daylight.

In the Codex

Climbs walls and turns hostile in the dark.

A spider ridden by a skeleton is the rarest bad luck.

What people say

A spider in daylight is only a neighbour. At dusk it remembers it is a hunter.

A hint

Its webs hang in neat rows, as if someone once taught it to.

Cave Spider

Abandoned mineshafts, around spawners.

In the Codex

Small, quick and poisonous. Found around spawners in mineshafts.

Burn the cobwebs first. They always come from the webs.

What people say

Miners keep a canary for the gas and a torch for the small ones.

A hint

Its poison is the miners' old fear, grown teeth.

Slime

Swamps at night; deep underground in fixed chunks; splits when hit.

In the Codex

Bounces in swamps by moonlight, and in certain chunks underground.

Slime chunks never move. The Seers called that proof the map never changes.

What people say

A slime is the ground's cough. It only wants to be somewhere else.

A hint

It lives where the world was patched. The seam never quite closed.

Silverfish

Strongholds, mountain stone.

In the Codex

Hides inside stone in strongholds and mountains. Hit one and the rest wake.

The vault-builders guarded the eye-door with pests, it seems.

What people say

Masons tap a stone twice before they trust it.

The End and the sky

Enderman

Everywhere at night, thick in the End and warped forests; carries blocks, teleports, hates water.

In the Codex

Don't meet its eyes. It takes that personally.

The Vitera say it is trying to remember who it was.

What people say

An enderman never takes the best block, only the one you meant to use.

A hint

It flinches from rain as from a name spoken aloud.

Endermite

Where an ender pearl lands.

In the Codex

Sometimes crawls out where an ender pearl lands. Endermen hate it.

Small, but it bites like a debt.

What people say

Pearl-throwers keep one eye on their boots.

A hint

Endermen hate it as one hates a mistake being pointed out.

Shulker

End-themed dungeon runs.

In the Codex

Hides in its shell and fires bullets that make you float.

The Ascended trusted them to guard their towers.

What people say

A shulker opens only to those it means to hurt.

A hint

Its bullets lift you off your feet, as if the End had its own idea of weight.

Phantom

The sky at night, after nights without sleep.

In the Codex

Comes for anyone who has not slept in three nights.

The Sleepless kept vigil to see the end coming. The phantoms came first.

What people say

Mothers say: sleep, or the sky will come for you.

A hint

They come for the sleepless, as the sleepless once came for the stars.

Ender Dragon

The End arena.

In the Codex

The beast at the end of everything. Its crystals heal it; break them first.

Every age has killed it. Every age, it came back.

What people say

Sailors say that at the end of the world something waits for you to arrive.

A hint

It does not guard the End. It waits there.

Illagers and the Witch

Pillager

Outposts, patrols, raids, woodland mansions.

In the Codex

Crossbow raider. Its captains drop ominous bottles that bring raids.

Grey faces, sour tongues: what is left of an empire, a scribe wrote.

What people say

A pillager hates a village for the same reason a beggar hates a bakery.

A hint

They raid in order, never at random, as if following an old list.

Vindicator

Woodland mansions, raids, patrols.

In the Codex

Axe-swinging raider of mansions and raids.

Name one Johnny and watch it lose its temper with everyone.

What people say

A vindicator never raids. He collects.

A hint

It carries an axe where a king once carried a pen.

Evoker

Woodland mansions, raids.

In the Codex

Calls fangs from the ground and vexes from the air. Drops a totem.

Its words were found in a vault no one built, an old scribe wrote.

What people say

An evoker's chanting is the only sound a village hears and cannot place.

A hint

Its totem turns death away once. Someone, long ago, wanted that to be possible.

Vex

Summoned by evokers.

In the Codex

Called by evokers. It flies through walls and dies on its own.

Small, winged and furious: a curse with a sword.

What people say

Wherever an evoker stands, the air gets crowded.

A hint

It lives a few moments and spends each one angry.

Ravager

Raids, patrols, outposts.

In the Codex

The battering ram of every raid. Block its charge with a shield.

The Hollow Kings rode them to war.

What people say

Villagers say a ravager has never been loved, only ridden.

A hint

Its horns could pass for a crown.

Witch

Swamp huts, raids.

In the Codex

Throws potions and drinks her own. Lives in swamp huts.

Descended from the Brewers, some say, minus the cures.

What people say

A witch is only a brewer nobody trusts.

A hint

She drinks her own work, so she knows the cure and will not share it.

The Nether

Blaze

Nether fortresses, spawners.

In the Codex

Spins and spits fire in Nether fortresses. Snowballs hurt it.

The fortress-builders called them sparks from a forge, and followed them home.

What people say

Nobody has seen a blaze rest.

A hint

Cold unmakes it, as if it were a fire borrowed from somewhere warmer than here.

Ghast

Nether wastes, soul sand valleys.

In the Codex

Wails and fires exploding fireballs. Hit them back.

Fortress diaries call them the crying sky.

What people say

Travellers who come back say the ghasts weep for the sky they lost.

A hint

Its tears burn. Its fireballs can be sent back, as if sorrow could be returned.

Happy Ghast

From a dried ghast, in the overworld.

In the Codex

Raised from a dried ghast. Fit it with a harness and fly.

Proof the Nether can be gentle, if you keep it wet.

What people say

Children call them clouds with manners.

A hint

It was never meant to be fierce. The dry made it angry.

Piglin

Nether, bastion remnants.

In the Codex

Trades for gold, and attacks anyone not wearing some.

Bastions were built by someone before them, the fortress diaries say.

What people say

Wear one gold boot and you are a guest. Wear none and you are dinner.

A hint

Their trade is older than their tongue. They remember the exchange, not the words.

Piglin Brute

Bastion remnants.

In the Codex

Guards bastion treasure with a golden axe. Gold won't calm it.

It never trades. It remembers what gold is for.

What people say

The brutes never smile and never sell.

A hint

Its axe is gold, and so is its anger.

Zombified Piglin

Nether wastes, and from lightning-struck pigs / piglins in the overworld.

In the Codex

Peaceful until you hit one. Then all of them remember.

A piglin brought to the overworld becomes this. The Nether doesn't travel well.

What people say

Strike one and the whole herd takes it personally.

A hint

It carries its golden sword like a prayer.

Hoglin

Crimson forests, bastions.

In the Codex

Charges through crimson forests. Warped fungus makes it back away.

Brought to the overworld, it turns into a zoglin.

What people say

Piglins breed them like cattle and fear them like kings.

A hint

Warped fungus scares it. It remembers a gentler wood.

Zoglin

The overworld, from hoglins.

In the Codex

A hoglin gone wrong in the overworld. Attacks everything.

The overworld does that to Nether things. Or the Nether to us.

What people say

A zoglin has no herd and no friend. Every shadow looks like a rival.

Strider

Lava lakes and seas in the Nether.

In the Codex

Walks on lava. Saddle it and steer with warped fungus on a stick.

Out of lava it shivers, as if it remembers being warm.

What people say

Strider herders say they hum when the lava is warm and sulk when it is not.

A hint

It shivers on dry ground as if the cold were a stranger.

Magma Cube

Basalt deltas, Nether fortresses.

In the Codex

Bounces through basalt deltas and splits when struck.

Frogs love them. That should tell you something.

What people say

Hard to say which is worse, the heat or the bouncing.

A hint

The slime of the Nether. Same bounce, hotter mind.

Wither

Summoned by the player.

In the Codex

Three heads, no mercy. Built from soul sand and wither skulls.

The fortress diaries call it a king. They locked his skull away.

What people say

Nobody has built one on purpose and meant well.

A hint

Three heads, as if a council had agreed on one thing.

It listens as if waiting for one particular sound.

Moves only when you look away, and cannot die while its heart beats.

Breeze

Trial chambers.

An ancient beast hatched from old eggs. It digs up seeds nobody has seen.

Its seeds come from a world that no longer exists.

What people say

Gardeners treasure a sniffer's find, and never ask where it dug.

Snow Golem

Ocean monuments.
